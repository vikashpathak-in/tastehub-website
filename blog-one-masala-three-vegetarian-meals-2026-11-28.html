One Onion-Tomato Masala Base, Three Easy Vegetarian Meals

Published 28 November 2026 · 6 min read

A single onion-tomato masala can become the starting point for several different vegetarian dishes. Instead of preparing a new base every evening, make one batch, divide it and finish each portion with a different main ingredient.

This TasteHub kitchen method is flexible. Adjust the chilli, salt and consistency to suit your household, and use vegetables that are available in season.

Ingredients for the base masala

  • 3 medium onions, finely chopped
  • 4 medium tomatoes, finely chopped
  • 2 tablespoons cooking oil
  • 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 1 tablespoon ginger-garlic paste
  • ½ teaspoon turmeric powder
  • 1 teaspoon coriander powder
  • Red chilli powder and salt, to taste
  • A little water as needed

Prepare the base

  1. Heat the oil in a heavy pan and add the cumin seeds.
  2. Add the onions and cook over medium heat until soft and lightly golden.
  3. Add the ginger-garlic paste and cook briefly, stirring so it does not stick.
  4. Add the tomatoes, turmeric, coriander powder, chilli and salt.
  5. Cook until the tomatoes soften and the mixture becomes thick. Stir regularly and add a small splash of water if the masala catches at the bottom.
  6. Switch off the heat and divide the base into three portions.

Meal 1: Chana masala

You need: one portion of the base, about 1½ cups cooked chickpeas and water as needed.

Add the chickpeas to the masala with enough water to make a curry. Simmer until the chickpeas are coated and the gravy reaches the consistency you like. Finish with chopped coriander or a squeeze of lemon if available. Serve with roti, rice or jeera rice.

Meal 2: Paneer curry

You need: one portion of the base, about 200 g paneer and approximately ½ cup water.

Warm the masala with water, then add paneer cubes. Simmer gently so the paneer warms through without breaking apart. Taste and adjust the seasoning. Serve with chapati, paratha or plain rice.

Meal 3: Mixed vegetable curry

You need: one portion of the base and about 2 cups mixed vegetables, cut into similar-sized pieces.

Add the vegetables to the masala. Add a little water, cover and cook until the vegetables are tender. If you are using vegetables with very different cooking times, add the firmer pieces first and the quicker-cooking pieces later. Serve with roti or rice.

Make each dish taste different

  • For chana, add roasted cumin or amchur if you enjoy a tangy flavour.
  • For paneer, finish with kasuri methi or a little garam masala.
  • For mixed vegetables, add peas, beans, carrot, cauliflower or seasonal produce according to availability.
  • Adjust the amount of water separately for each dish. A thick masala for roti may need a lighter consistency for rice.

Batch preparation and kitchen care

Prepare only the quantity you can handle safely. If the base is made ahead, divide it into clean, covered, shallow containers and refrigerate it promptly. FSSAI's Food Safety at Home guide advises keeping food prepared in advance or leftovers cold below 5°C and reheating cooked food thoroughly before eating. Follow safe handling practices and do not leave cooked food sitting out for extended periods.

TasteHub kitchen note

One base does not have to mean one repeated meal. Change the main ingredient, texture and finishing flavours, and you can make three familiar vegetarian dishes from the same starting point.
